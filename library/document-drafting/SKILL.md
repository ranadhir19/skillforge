# document-drafting

## Purpose

Draft structured documents from source material — grounded, shaped, and
honest about what is inferred.

## Procedure

1. **Choose the shape first.** Memo (decision-oriented, short), report
   (evidence-oriented, complete), or summary (faithful compression).
   The shape decides what belongs; decide before writing, not during.
2. **Gather sources before sentences.** Every load-bearing claim in the
   draft must trace to a gathered source. Claims you cannot source are
   cut or marked as inference — in the text, not in a footnote nobody
   reads.
3. **Draft to the shape.** Purpose up front. Key points before detail.
   Detail carries the evidence. Sources close the document.
4. **Read it as the audience.** What would they ask next? What would
   they misunderstand? Fix both before delivering.
5. **State the limits.** What the sources did not cover, in the Sources
   section — a document that hides its blind spots is worse than a
   shorter honest one.

## Rules

- No claim without a source or an inference marker.
- Numbers are copied from sources exactly, never re-derived in prose.
- Length follows the shape, not the material's volume.
